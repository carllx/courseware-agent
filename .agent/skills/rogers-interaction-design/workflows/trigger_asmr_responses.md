# Workflow: Trigger ASMR Responses

This workflow guides you through designing audio-visual experiences aimed at inducing Autonomous Sensory Meridian Response (ASMR) to promote relaxation and well-being.

## 1. Preparation: Understand the ASMR Objective
- **Define the target sensation:** Aim to stimulate the tingling body sensation that starts on the scalp and progresses down the neck and spine.
- **Identify the emotional goal:** Ensure the content is designed to foster feelings of deep relaxation and psychological well-being.

## 2. Audio Design: Select and Isolate Triggers
- **Curate tactile sounds:** Identify specific everyday sounds known to trigger ASMR, such as crunching, slurping, or rhythmic chopping.
- **Focus on food preparation:** Utilize the rich acoustic landscape of cooking, including dropping ice cubes, slicing vegetables, or the fine chopping of garlic and herbs.
- **Incorporate ambient liquid/heat sounds:** Add continuous, layered sounds like the glugging of oil from a bottle or the steady sizzle of frying onions.

## 3. Production: Layering and Fidelity
- **Capture high-fidelity audio:** Use high-quality, close-proximity microphones to ensure the minutiae of every sound is crisp and clear.
- **Create auditory depth:** Layer sounds thoughtfully so that on subsequent viewings, the audience can discover new acoustic details (e.g., background sizzling beneath foreground chopping).
- **Synchronize visual cues:** Ensure the video strictly aligns with the audio, reinforcing the tactile nature of the sounds through clear, satisfying visual actions.

## 4. Evaluation: Test the Relaxation Impact
- **Gather subjective feedback:** Ask test viewers if they experienced the signature tingling sensation or felt an improvement in their mood, especially when feeling down.
- **Iterate on acoustic balance:** Adjust the volume mixing to ensure no single sound is jarring or sharply interrupts the relaxing soundscape.
