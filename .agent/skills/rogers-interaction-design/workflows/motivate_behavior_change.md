# Workflow: Motivate Behavior Change

This workflow details how to design interactive, emotionally resonant systems that motivate users to build and sustain positive habits, such as physical activity or sustainable practices.

## 1. Concept: Establish the Core Motivation
- **Identify the target habit:** Define the specific behavior you want to encourage (e.g., exercising daily, using stairs instead of escalators, recycling).
- **Choose the motivational framework:** Decide whether to use emotional attachment (like caring for a virtual pet) or environmental playfulness (like a piano staircase).

## 2. Design: Create the Reward and Consequence System
- **Define the metric for success:** Establish clear, trackable actions (e.g., 20 steps equal 1 reward credit or "watt").
- **Implement positive reinforcement:** Provide tangible or digital rewards (like buying virtual presents) when the user consistently performs the desired behavior.
- **Incorporate mild negative feedback (sulking):** Design the system to react visibly when behavior drops off (e.g., a virtual pet becoming angry or refusing to play after a week of inactivity).

## 3. Execution: Foster Emotional and Playful Engagement
- **Build emotional attachment:** Design characters or interfaces that require the user's direct action to thrive, leveraging the human tendency to care for dependents.
- **Integrate playfulness:** Transform mundane tasks into fun experiences, using auditory or visual feedback (e.g., making an outdoor bin make a cartoonish falling sound to encourage trash disposal).
- **Ensure consistent tracking:** Use reliable sensors (like pedometers) so the user trusts the system accurately reflects their effort.

## 4. Evaluation: Analyze Long-Term Adoption
- **Measure consistent engagement:** Evaluate if the playful or emotional hook keeps the user motivated over a continuous period, rather than just a short-term novelty phase.
- **Assess behavioral impact:** Confirm that the user's real-world habits (e.g., increased physical activity) have genuinely improved.
- **Iterate the challenge:** As the behavior becomes a habit, subtly adjust the difficulty or introduce new rewards to prevent stagnation and maintain interest.
